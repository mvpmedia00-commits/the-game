# MVP Media: Las Vegas — The Underground Cut (Explicit)

*Own the frame. Then own the film.*

You're **Matthew "MVP" Phillips** — red beret, red suit, gold buckle, DSLR in hand. You start in room 12 of the Desert Rose Motel with a borrowed lens and a phone that won't stop ringing. You end up running the biggest adult studio on the Strip.

The paperwork is **invisible**. Nobody stops the action to check an ID or wave a contract around. It's just true in the background — assumed, automatic, part of the world. The game spends its time on the shit you can actually *feel*: the shot, the drive, the deal, the money.

---

## The climb

You start as a **hired lens** and end as a **producer who owns his masters**. Seven rungs, no filler:

1. **Vendor.** Tony Velvet calls, you shoot Ruby's headliner set, you get paid. You're someone else's camera.
2. **Runner.** You race proofs to an editor and chase a paparazzo who stole your cards.
3. **Byline.** You land a swimwear cover with Nova Starr. Your name is on it now.
4. **Landlord.** You buy a dead soundstage. A room is where films get made.
5. **Producer.** Jade Rivers — veteran performer *and* director, your mentor — walks you through your first feature. You cast, you set the stage, you call the shots, you wrap.
6. **Distributor.** Your film gets bootlegged. You hunt the van. Now you defend your own IP.
7. **Mogul.** You shoot the premiere of your own movie while your rival makes a last grab for the master drive. You end the night owning the frame, the film, and the future.

---

## The business, stripped to what matters

No red tape on screen. Just the levers that make a studio run:

- **Talent roster.** Ruby, Nova, Jade, Coco, Lex — each with a star rating, a day rate, and a taste (neon, warm, dramatic, playful). You *sign* them. They become your stable, your brand, your recurring cast.
- **Productions.** One film a day. Cast up to two signed performers, pick a set, pick a crew tier, make director's calls on lighting and mood. Match a performer's taste for bonus quality.
- **Royalties.** Quality drives opening sales. Opening sales pay **8% every day after**. You're not making one film — you're building a catalog that pays while you sleep.
- **Rep (0–100).** The currency of access. Raises pay, unlocks talent, gates the good jobs.

Every performer is a named adult professional with a paid day rate. That's the only rule that matters, and it's automatic. Scenes end at **"Cut! That's a wrap."** and nobody broke stride to file a form.

---

## The explicit part

Here's where the gloves come off. The game doesn't hide what the business *is* — it just doesn't stop to explain it.

**The roster is your stable of performers.** These aren't models who happen to do films. They're working adult talent with bodies, personalities and appetites, and the game treats them like it. Ruby's a headliner who knows exactly what she's worth and makes you earn the shot. Nova wants covers and knows the camera loves her body. Jade's a five-star veteran who's seen every set, every ego, every bullshit move — and runs hers like a general. Coco works burlesque and brings that tease to everything. Lex runs her own company and signs *only* with people who don't waste her time.

**Productions are the payoff.** When you own the studio and cast two signed performers, you're not just picking stats — you're putting two specific people on a set, under lights you chose, in a mood you set, and the quality score is the difference between a forgettable shoot and something that sells. Match their taste and the scene *works*. Miss it and you get a flat, cheap-looking film that opens soft and dies at the box office.

**The sets are the fantasy.** Stage A with the cyclorama and softboxes is your bread and butter. The Desert Rose pool at night is glossy, wet, warm — Nova's dream. The Velvet Room after hours is red velvet and neon, all drama and shadow. The Crown penthouse is money, glass, skyline. Each one is a *look*, and the look is the product.

**The money is real.** Opening sales are quality × (25 + rep) × 2.2, and then **8% of that lands in your account every single day after**. Stack films, stack royalties, stack rep, and you go from counting hundreds to counting thousands without lifting the camera. That's the whole arc: stop renting your talent out to other people's productions and start owning the catalog.

**The rival is a pirate.** Silas Crane and the Neon Kings steal your masters and sell your films behind your back. The back half of the story is a turf war over *who owns the work* — the most real tension in any creative business, adult or otherwise.

---

## What it feels like

- **The camera is the whole game.** Composition, framing, timing, gear — every shot graded A+ to D. You're a photographer first, and everything is seen through the viewfinder.
- **The phone is your office.** Story, gigs, roster, productions, save. You live on that screen between sets.
- **The city is small and dense on purpose.** 600 m of boulevard, 500 m of Fremont, wet streets, traffic, pedestrians, a clock that never stops.
- **The vibe is after-hours.** 11:40 PM, neon bleeding into puddles, a night that refuses to end.

---

## The tone

Mature, explicit in *attitude* if not in pixels — but the industry is texture, not homework. MVP red and gold against neon pink, teal and amber. Bloom on every sign, softbox glow on a cyclorama, wet asphalt reflecting the whole damn city. *Boogie Nights* energy, business-sim spine, shot through a DSLR.

You're not playing a performer. You're playing the guy behind the camera who realized the camera was the way in — and built an empire out of it.
